Start here: two subjects, one connected system

A business may want to raise money against a portfolio of assets. An investor may want to buy that exposure, receive income and later sell it. A payment user may simply want to move a currency-denominated amount between wallets. RWA tokenization concerns what is being owned or financed. Stablecoins concern one possible form of money used to pay for it. Understanding one helps you understand the other, but their rights and risks differ.

This guide follows the teaching subjects: stablecoins in selected Asian jurisdictions, and tokenization/RWAs in Hong Kong, Mainland China and Singapore. It begins without assumed blockchain or finance knowledge. The advanced chapters then examine legal structuring, technical architecture, custody, insolvency and recovery. Asia is the centre of the discussion; Japan and international research provide comparisons rather than a country-by-country survey of every Asian legal system.

Research cut-off: 27 November 2026. Rules, proposals, operational launches and historical commentary are labelled separately. Public educational material, not a legal opinion on a particular issuance. Examples and numerical simulations are invented teaching scenarios unless expressly identified as real developments.

Read the foundation chapters in order. For professional preparation, then read the three jurisdiction chapters, the technical chapter, the cases and the annotated reading room. Every bracketed source link opens a source record; every record links directly to the publisher. The Chinese reading pathway provides original-language material and explains why the wording matters.

The building blocks: ledgers, wallets and tokens

A ledger is a record of who holds what. A bank’s account database is a ledger. A company’s register of members is another. A distributed ledger is maintained across several machines under common rules. A blockchain is one form of distributed ledger in which records are grouped into cryptographically linked blocks. A shared record can reduce reconciliation between participants, but the way it reaches agreement and admits participants determines its security and governance.

A wallet is software or hardware used to manage signing authority and interact with a ledger. Tokens are recorded on that ledger; they are not little files physically stored inside a phone. A private key authorises transactions, while an address identifies a destination or account. A custodian may hold the signing authority for a customer. A customer’s exchange balance may instead be an entry in the exchange’s own database, backed by pooled on-chain assets. These arrangements produce different legal and operational risks.

A token is a digital record governed by a ledger or program. Fungible units are interchangeable within the relevant class; a non-fungible token identifies a distinct item or record. A token representing one fund unit does not become the same asset as a token representing one dollar. Symbols and logos can be copied. Identify a token by its network and contract address, then verify the issuer and terms.

A smart contract is executable code. It can update balances, enforce transfer conditions and release assets. Its name does not guarantee that it is a legally enforceable contract, that it is intelligent, or that it understands facts outside the ledger. An oracle brings in external information such as an interest rate or asset value. Someone must choose, maintain and, where necessary, correct that information.

A public chain may allow broad participation at the network layer while a particular security token restricts transfers to approved holders. A permissioned network restricts participation more directly. Neither architecture by itself settles investor eligibility, asset ownership, privacy compliance or what happens if the network stops. When reading a project proposal, ask separately who may validate, who may see data, who may transact, and who may change the code.

A map of digital money

People often use “digital currency” as if it described a single thing. Start instead with who owes the holder what. Your bank deposit is ordinarily a claim against a bank. A non-bank stablecoin is a different issuer’s instrument. A tokenized fund is an investment interest. Bitcoin is not a promise by a central issuer to redeem at a fixed currency value. These differences remain even if all appear in the same wallet.

“Backed by dollars” is not enough. Does the holder own a beneficial interest in segregated reserves, have a direct redemption right, or merely have a contractual claim against an intermediary? Does the relevant bank accept the token as a deposit? Is statutory deposit protection available to this holder and arrangement? Do not infer these answers from marketing language.

The settlement asset matters when buying a tokenized bond. If the bond arrives but the payment token cannot be redeemed, technical completion has not delivered the cash outcome the investor expected. Conversely, a highly reliable payment rail cannot repair a defective claim to the bond. This connects the money chapter to the RWA legal structure and technical settlement chapters.

China-specific correction: do not teach the 2021 e-CNY design description as a timeless definition of every 2026 wallet. Bank notices introduced interest on real-name digital-renminbi wallets from 1 January 2026, and official 2026 explanations describe distinct retail bank-deposit-money and wholesale central-bank-settlement layers. Read the current terms of the particular wallet and do not equate e-CNY with a privately issued offshore RMB stablecoin.

Stablecoins: issuance, redemption and the peg

A stablecoin aims to hold a reference value, commonly one unit of a fiat currency. The target price, the market price and the legal redemption amount are three separate things. A token can trade below its target even when an issuer says it has sufficient reserves. A secondary-market buyer may face eligibility checks, minimum redemption sizes, fees, delays or intermediaries before reaching the issuer.

In a simple fiat-reserve design, an eligible customer sends conventional money to the issuer. After funds are confirmed, the issuer mints tokens. Holders transfer them among wallets or platforms. For redemption, eligible tokens are returned or otherwise locked/burned under the issuer’s process, and conventional money is paid out. The sequencing of these steps matters: minting before settled funds arrive creates exposure, while burning before a failed payout creates a reconciliation problem requiring a recoverable claim.

Arbitrage is the attempt to exploit a price difference. In an invented example, buying a token for 0.98 currency units and redeeming it for 1.00 leaves a gross difference of 0.02. The opportunity shrinks after fees, financing, delays and the chance redemption fails. When access is broad and credible, arbitrage can help pull the market price towards par. It is not a guarantee during a run. Empirical work by Lyons and Viswanath-Natraj studies how issuance/redemption arrangements affect stablecoin pricing.

Three broad designs deserve separate treatment. A fiat-reserve coin relies on external reserve assets and an issuer/custodian structure. A crypto-collateralized coin uses volatile on-chain collateral, often with excess collateral and liquidation rules. An algorithmic or reflexive design may rely heavily on incentives and related tokens. Hybrid arrangements exist. A stable label says little about which risk mechanism actually supports it.

For a reserve-backed instrument, investigate reserve quality, concentration, maturity, currency matching, segregation, valuation and access during stress. A nominally sufficient portfolio may not produce immediately available cash. Reserve reporting should be read for its date, methodology, scope and assurance level. An attestation to a balance on one date is different from an audit of the whole business and does not itself establish holders’ legal priority.

A run can become self-reinforcing: doubts cause redemptions; redemptions require asset sales; sales may crystallise losses or expose operational bottlenecks; further doubts follow. Holding safer assets can reduce that risk but affect an issuer’s earnings. The IMF’s 2026 model explicitly explores this stability/profitability trade-off. It is a model of incentives, not a product-rating system.

Stablecoin payments also have a full journey: acquiring funds, compliance checks, choosing the correct network, paying any fees, transferring, waiting for the required finality, and converting to the recipient’s desired money. Advertised chain speed covers only part of that journey. Compare the complete cost and time with a bank transfer or fast-payment system before claiming an improvement.

Explore reserve stress and redemption access

This is a deliberately simplified teaching model, not a forecast or a solvency test. Assume 100 million currency units of tokens, 20 million cash and 80 million securities. Adjust a securities haircut and an immediate redemption request. The first output asks whether the marked reserve value covers all tokens; the second asks whether cash plus haircut-adjusted security sales can meet that request. Neither output proves legal access to reserves.

A reserve value above the requested payout is not enough to conclude full solvency. Compare it with all outstanding tokens. A portfolio may also be solvent at hold-to-maturity values but illiquid today. Real structures require asset eligibility, settlement timing, liquidity facilities, operational capacity, creditor priority and currency matching. This demo intentionally omits those complications so the distinction is visible.

Worked example and interpretation

At a 5% haircut, the securities realise 76 million and total reserves realise 96 million. An immediate request for 30 million can be met in this model using 20 million cash and selling enough securities to raise another 10 million. Yet the issuer has a 4 million shortfall against total token liabilities. Paying early redeemers at par does not make the remaining holders whole. The question “can I redeem now?” is different from “can everybody redeem in full?”

RWA tokenization: what the token actually represents

RWA means “real-world asset” in industry usage. The category usually includes traditional financial assets as well as physical assets: government bonds, fund units, private loans, receivables, property interests, commodities and contractual cash flows. “Real-world” does not mean low risk, legally perfected or independently verified.

Tokenization creates or records a representation of an asset or claim on a programmable platform. Sometimes the token is integral to issuance and the authoritative register. Sometimes it mirrors an existing register. Sometimes it represents an interest in a special-purpose vehicle (SPV) that owns something else. The legal chain between the holder and the underlying asset is the central investigation.

Consider a hypothetical apartment building. A company owns the land and building. Investors receive tokens representing shares in that company, or notes issued by it, or contractual revenue participation. Those are three different arrangements. Token transfer does not automatically update the land register, discharge a mortgage, create a valid trust or give the holder a right to occupy the building. A small denomination makes an investment easier to divide; it does not make it easy to sell.

For a tokenized bond, the issuer owes principal and interest on specified terms. For a tokenized fund, an investor holds an interest subject to the fund’s portfolio, valuation and redemption arrangements. For a tokenized receivable, the investor must ask whether the debtor’s obligation was validly assigned, whether notice or consent was required, and who can enforce it. For a gold token, investigate the vault, allocation, insurance, redemption conditions and legal title.

Native versus mirrored: a digitally native instrument is issued through the digital arrangement itself; a mirrored token may reference an independently constituted asset. Neither description answers the legal question by itself. The documents must specify the authoritative ownership record, conditions of valid transfer, correction procedure, and precedence when records disagree. A “digital twin” without enforceable rights can be only information.

The BIS tokenisation continuum highlights a useful tension: assets already easy to standardise may offer modest gains per transaction, while fragmented and legally complex assets can be hardest to tokenize despite larger hoped-for gains. Use that as a project-selection question. What existing reconciliation, settlement or servicing problem is expensive enough to justify the new architecture?

Follow one tokenized asset through its whole life

The following is an original analytical workflow for evaluating a hypothetical regulated issuance. Each stage should produce evidence, not merely a diagram. A token is only one component; people, institutions and legal documents perform many of the consequential steps.

  1. Select and verify the asset. Identify the owner, debtor, encumbrances, eligibility, valuation and cash-flow history. For receivables, test whether invoices are genuine, already financed or subject to set-off. For property, inspect the registry and restrictions.
  2. Build the legal vehicle and entitlement. Decide whether the investor receives a debt, share, fund interest, trust interest or another claim. Document the asset transfer, perfection steps, priority, governing law, jurisdiction and enforcement rights.
  3. Classify activities and distribution. Work through issuer, arranger, broker, platform, fund manager, custodian and payment roles in each jurisdiction. Product authorisation, intermediary licensing, offering exemptions and advertising restrictions are distinct questions.
  4. Design the digital register and controls. Specify the authoritative record; investor identity; whitelisting; transfer limits; mint/burn authority; suspension; correction; upgrade and recovery powers. Agree what happens if a key is compromised.
  5. Onboard and issue. Complete identity, eligibility, AML and source-of-funds checks. Match subscription money to the investor. Mint only under an authorised process, with a reconciliation trail between tokens and enforceable claims.
  6. Settle and hold. Determine the payment asset and whether delivery and payment are conditional on one another. Record finality and custody responsibilities. Check whether transfers to an omnibus address change the investor’s legal relationship.
  7. Service. Collect underlying cash, deduct authorised fees and distribute income. Process corporate actions, voting, disclosures, valuations, tax reporting and updated investor information. An oracle can carry a number; someone remains responsible for its accuracy.
  8. Exit, maturity or default. Support only permitted secondary trading. Reconcile redemption to destruction or immobilisation of tokens. For default, invoke contractual remedies and use the legal and technical control map. End-of-life reconciliation should prevent duplicate claims.

Follow the cash separately. A loan debtor paying an SPV’s bank account is not the same event as a smart contract paying token holders. Identify the collection agent, account control, remittance deadlines, deductions and replacement servicer. If collections stop, a perfectly functioning token contract may faithfully distribute nothing.

Ask for a failure rehearsal. What happens if the custodian becomes insolvent, an administrator vanishes, valuations are contested, a chain forks, personal data are exposed, or an investor loses a key? Responsibilities should be assigned before those events. “The community will decide” is not a complete governance policy for a regulated instrument.

Economics: yield, liquidity and the investment underneath

Tokenization changes how a claim is represented, transferred and serviced. The investment still has ordinary economics. A government bond has interest-rate and sovereign risks. A private credit token has borrower and recovery risks. A property vehicle has rents, vacancy, maintenance, leverage and sale costs. A carbon-credit token requires scrutiny of the underlying standard, registry, retirement process and double-counting risk.

Work from gross asset cash flow to investor cash flow. In an invented annual example, a portfolio collects 8 million, incurs 1 million servicing/operating costs and 2 million credit losses, and pays 1 million senior financing costs. Only 4 million remains before any further fees and allocation to the relevant investors. An advertised 8% asset yield would not be an 8% return to an investor whose capital bears all those deductions.

A secondary price can move independently of the accounting value. A token with a reported net asset value of 1.00 might trade at 0.93 because buyers expect delays, have eligibility costs or distrust the valuation. Conversely, a liquid token does not necessarily allow quick redemption from an illiquid underlying portfolio. Separate market liquidity (selling to another person), funding liquidity (obtaining cash), and asset liquidity (selling the underlying asset without a steep discount).

Continuous trading and periodic valuation: a fund token can trade while its underlying market is closed. A market maker may charge a wider spread to bear that uncertainty. The published NAV may be stale. Before describing 24/7 access as better pricing, ask when the asset is valued, who provides liquidity, how that provider hedges, and what happens during suspension.

Project evaluation should compare total lifetime costs: legal structuring, licences, investor onboarding, custody, platform fees, cybersecurity, reconciliation, reporting and wind-down. Count actual reduced errors and processing costs. Do not count expected secondary liquidity as a realised saving before a market exists. The IMF’s October 2026 financial-stability chapter provides current research context on efficiencies and new vulnerabilities.

Technical reading prompt: instantaneous settlement versus netting

Atomic gross settlement can reduce principal exposure but require cash or collateral earlier. Traditional netting can economise on funding while leaving exposure during the interval before settlement. A proposal must compare counterparty-risk savings against prefunding, liquidity fragmentation and loss of netting benefits. Faster settlement is an engineering property; lower total risk is a conclusion requiring a wider assessment.

Hong Kong: stablecoin money and tokenized investments

Hong Kong uses different regulatory tracks for different functions. The HKMA administers the stablecoin issuer regime. The SFC supervises relevant securities, investment products and regulated intermediary/platform activities. A licence in one track is not permission to perform every role in the other. Begin with the claim and activity, then identify the applicable authorisation.

Stablecoins: an operational licensing regime

The Stablecoins Ordinance regime commenced on 1 August 2025. Its perimeter includes specified fiat-referenced issuance with a Hong Kong nexus; apply the statutory definitions rather than treating every digital token alike. The HKMA portal links licensing, supervision and AML/CFT guidance. Important review areas include full reserve backing, segregation, redemption, governance, risk management and technology controls.

The official register checked for this guide lists Anchorpoint Financial Limited (FRS01) and The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation Limited (FRS02), each with an effective licence date of 10 April 2026. A licence is not proof that a particular product has launched, is offered to every investor, or guarantees redemption in every circumstance. Consult the live register and the issuer’s actual terms.

Tokenized investments: look beyond the token label

A token representing a security remains subject to the relevant securities framework. A digital format alone does not establish whether the product is complex, suitable for retail distribution or eligible for a particular venue. Analyse the underlying instrument, tokenization features, custody model and distribution route together.

The SFC’s 20 April 2026 secondary-trading circular sets requirements principally designed for on-platform secondary trading of tokenized authorised open-ended funds. It addresses the trading channel, fair pricing, liquidity provision, disclosure and onboarding. It must be read alongside the other tokenization circulars; it is not general permission to list every RWA token.

By 26 October 2026, the SFC had announced the launch of 24/7 secondary trading of tokenized funds. Thus, a November 2026 lecture should not describe Hong Kong solely in terms of proposed or experimental secondary trading. The existence of operational funds still does not prove deep liquidity across all products.

A practitioner’s issue map

For a hypothetical HK-distributed fund token, ask who is the product provider, who holds the underlying portfolio, who maintains the fund register, who operates the trading channel, and who has mint/burn and recovery powers. Compare the investor’s direct register status with any nominee arrangement. Establish who makes the investor whole if an erroneous token transfer conflicts with the authoritative register.

For an HK-dollar settlement token, separately examine issuer licensing, redemption rights, reserve arrangements and where the token is offered. For an asset originating in Mainland China, Hong Kong’s rules are only part of the analysis: Mainland restrictions, cross-border controls and any required filing remain relevant. A Hong Kong licence is not a Mainland distribution permission.

Hong Kong in 2026: rules becoming operating infrastructure

Read the binding and supervisory language carefully

The HKMA’s Chinese supervision guideline distinguishes statutory wording from supervisory expectations. It addresses reserve-market-value coverage, effective trust segregation, qualified custody, rights on insolvency and redemption at par. Under paragraph 3.3.3, valid requests should generally be processed within one business day after receipt unless otherwise approved; this is not an unconditional guarantee that every new wallet can bypass onboarding and compliance checks. Section 2.6 prohibits the licensee paying or permitting interest on its specified stablecoins, while distinguishing non-interest marketing incentives. Reserve-asset earnings and losses belong to the licensee. Read sections 2–3 with the issuer’s actual terms.

[HKGUIDE]

The revised SFC product circular dated 20 April 2026 retains provider responsibility despite outsourcing and requires clear ownership records, settlement finality and suitable technology controls. The November 2023 intermediary circular remains relevant but is a different document. The May 2026 stablecoin-services circular provides tailored treatment for defined relevant stablecoins from HKMA licensees; its scope should not be extended to every token describing itself as stable.

Mainland China: the 2026 boundary is essential

“China” must not be used as shorthand for one uniform digital-asset regime. Mainland China and Hong Kong have distinct financial regulatory frameworks. Technology development, digital-renminbi arrangements and a token sold offshore are different subjects. A news story about a successful offshore financing does not establish a generally available onshore route.

The eight-agency notice of 6 February 2026

The Chinese source is 《关于进一步防范和处置虚拟货币等相关风险的通知》, 银发〔2026〕42号. It expressly addresses RWA tokenization, preserves a restrictive approach to related financial activities, and contains an exception tied to approved activity and specified financial infrastructure. It also addresses offshore activities involving domestic interests and RMB-linked stablecoins. The 2021 notice is superseded; quoting only that older notice misses the new RWA provisions.

A separate, controlled offshore ABS-token route

The CSRC’s same-day 《关于境内资产境外发行资产支持证券代币的监管指引》, 公告〔2026〕1号, addresses overseas issuance of asset-backed securities tokens based on domestic assets/cash flows. It requires the relevant domestic actual controller to file before the issuance business and sets conditions and reporting obligations. The existence of this route does not convert domestic public crypto fundraising into a permitted activity. Filing also does not replace applicable foreign-exchange, cross-border investment, cybersecurity, data or other approvals.

Interpretation for a deal team: read the notice and the CSRC guidance together. First classify the underlying claim and activity; then establish whether the proposed structure fits the controlled route, an approved infrastructure arrangement, or prohibited activity. Identify each domestic participant and the ultimate economic exposure. A shell company offshore does not remove every domestic nexus.

e-CNY is a separate analysis

Real-name digital-renminbi wallet interest introduced in 2026 and the official description of retail/wholesale layers require care in teaching. The policy material does not mean that any private firm may issue a freely circulating RMB stablecoin, nor that every wallet has identical terms. Explain issuer, holder relationship, interest treatment and access conditions for the specific arrangement.

Chinese-language precision

“现实世界资产代币化” is a regulatory expression, not a promise that an asset has been legally transferred. “备案” means filing; it should not be casually translated as a universal licence. “境内资产境外发行” keeps the domestic origin and overseas issuance in the same analytical frame. “经批准” requires the actual approval, scope and responsible authority to be identified.

For a classroom hypothetical involving Mainland receivables and offshore investors, write two legal maps. One covers asset creation, assignment, servicing, data and money movement inside the Mainland. The other covers the offshore issuer, investor rights, distribution and settlement. The connection between them must be lawful and enforceable; a blockchain only records the chosen structure.

Singapore: separate the framework, legislation and trials

Singapore’s discussion combines digital-payment-token regulation, capital-markets regulation, a stablecoin policy framework and institutional tokenization initiatives. The same business may need more than one analysis. A payment-service permission is not a fund authorisation or a securities offering exemption.

Stablecoin status at this guide’s cut-off

MAS’s 1 September 2026 consultation proposed Payment Services Act amendments to implement the single-currency stablecoin framework. It also proposed provisions on joint multi-jurisdictional issuance, recognition of certain foreign-issued stablecoins, interest, stress testing, recovery and orderly wind-down. The consultation closed on 16 October 2026. Its closure does not make the proposals enacted law.

Accordingly, do not say that the 2023 policy announcement by itself already created a fully operating statutory stablecoin licensing category. Current official consumer guidance still distinguishes the framework from the legislative work needed for it to take effect. For an actual product, check the legislation’s commencement, licence class, issuer status and permitted designation at the time of the transaction.

Guardian and tokenized collateral

MAS Guardian brings public and private participants together on tokenized financial products, including funds, bonds and forms of money. The November 2026 collateral framework examines implementation questions such as entitlement records, custody, valuation, margin administration and default handling. The frameworks are valuable design references, but not approvals for every depicted structure.

A Singapore fund-unit token requires an analysis of the investment interest, offer, manager, custody and trading activity. Its transfer restrictions should reflect investor eligibility. A token used as collateral needs additional answers: how is security created and perfected, who has control, how is margin valued, and what can happen at default? Code that moves a token must be connected to the relevant legal collateral rights.

For a regional comparison, Hong Kong offers an operating stablecoin issuer licensing regime and a 2026 authorised-fund secondary-trading framework. Singapore’s 2026 stablecoin statutory implementation remains a proposal in the materials verified here, while institutional tokenization work continues. Mainland China’s restrictive perimeter and controlled offshore route form a different model. Avoid a single ranking of which jurisdiction is “most crypto-friendly”; match the actual activity to the rule.

Asia and the international context

Japan offers a useful comparison because fiat-linked electronic payment instruments have their own regulatory treatment. The FSA’s current FAQ explains issuer routes through regulated institutional categories and registration requirements for relevant sale/exchange services. A stablecoin intermediary and a cryptoasset exchange are not interchangeable labels. The FSA’s travel-rule material also illustrates how information obligations accompany covered transfers. Check current law and the exact category rather than assuming that a token accepted abroad may be distributed in Japan.

Elsewhere in Asia: the same comparative questions apply to Korea, Taiwan, ASEAN markets and the Gulf: is the token a payment instrument, security, fund interest, deposit or another claim; who may issue and distribute it; what cross-border and currency controls apply; and how will disputes and insolvency be handled? This guide does not claim a fully verified November 2026 statutory survey for every one of those markets. The core teaching scope remains Mainland China, Hong Kong and Singapore, with Japan as a checked comparator.

International policy: tokenization connects local markets to global settlement and liquidity arrangements. The BIS argues for strong monetary anchors and a shared programmable environment for central-bank money, commercial-bank money and financial assets. This is an institutional policy argument, not a statement that every useful arrangement must have one universal blockchain. The IMF emphasises that code, governance and settlement design can reallocate rather than eliminate trust.

Currency substitution deserves attention in Asia. A person may choose a foreign-currency stablecoin for access to that currency, rather than because blockchain technology itself is essential. A cross-border merchant may value predictable settlement but still need compliant conversion into local money. Examine exchange-rate risk, capital controls, consumer recourse and tax/accounting treatment along the complete transaction chain.

Global comparisons should be date-specific. EU, US and other regimes can affect Asia-based participants through the activities and markets they serve. Do not infer a foreign exemption from a Hong Kong licence. For this guide, current Asian primary sources take precedence over an older commentator’s global summary when they conflict.

Technical reading: architecture, controls and failure modes

This chapter assumes the earlier chapters are understood. The objective is to read a technical proposal intelligently, not to deploy an investment product. Separate the asset layer (the enforceable claim), record layer (authoritative entitlements), execution layer (contracts and transactions), money layer (settlement asset) and governance layer (who can intervene). A diagram with only wallets and arrows omits most of this architecture.

Token standards are interfaces

ERC-20 standardises fungible-token balances, transfers and allowances. ERC-3643 adds interfaces for identity, compliance checks and lifecycle/agent controls, including freezing, recovery and forced transfers. ERC-4626 standardises tokenized vault shares against an underlying token, with conversion and preview functions. These specifications help applications interoperate. None supplies the underlying legal claim or certifies compliance with a jurisdiction’s law.

When inspecting an implementation, identify the actual deployed address, source verification, proxy pattern and administrator. A published code repository may differ from the deployed bytecode. An audit may cover only a version or selected contracts. Check which role can mint, burn, pause, upgrade, change identity rules, replace an oracle or recover tokens. Evaluate the compromise of each such role separately.

Delivery versus payment and atomicity

Delivery versus payment (DvP) conditions asset delivery on payment. On a single compatible execution environment, one transaction can be designed to update both legs or revert them together. But bank money off-chain, multiple chains, delayed messages and custodian book entries can break that simple model. Technical finality, legal finality and practical redemption access must be analysed separately.

Settlement experiment: change payment availability and investor eligibility. This illustrative system uses atomic on-chain DvP. It demonstrates the expected state transition, not production code or a legal conclusion.

Before: seller holds the bond; buyer holds the payment.

The toy outcome assumes a single atomic transaction. In a real workflow, reserve the asset, test controls, establish a valid quote, execute the transaction, check required finality and reconcile both ledgers. If payment is a stablecoin, the issuer’s later redemption performance is still a separate exposure.

Oracles, bridges and asynchronous work

An oracle’s signed value can be authentic but stale or based on a poor methodology. A bridge can create a representation of a token on another chain, adding custody, messaging or validation assumptions. Two token symbols on different networks may have different redemption routes. A tokenized fund that takes days to process underlying redemptions cannot promise synchronous cash settlement merely by adopting a vault interface.

Privacy and identity

Public addresses can become linkable to people or institutions. Store only what is necessary on a persistent public ledger. Consider off-chain identity records and verifiable eligibility claims, with governance for expiry and revocation. Zero-knowledge methods can prove a selected fact without revealing all source data, but require a precise threat model and trustworthy implementation. “Encrypted” does not answer who may decrypt, how long data persists or how legal correction rights operate.

Controls, testing and operations

A technical assurance plan should test unauthorised minting, duplicated claims, replayed messages, decimal/rounding errors, compromised administrators, stale prices, transfer-rule changes, chain interruption and recovery. Separate duties and multi-party authorisation can reduce single-key risk; they also require an available and accountable operating team. Keep monitoring, incident response, external evidence and communication procedures outside the happy-path transaction code.

// Educational transaction specification, not executable code
verify entitlement and authoritative register
verify seller authority and buyer eligibility
verify asset amount, payment amount, expiry and unique instruction
if either leg cannot complete: change neither ledger state
otherwise: transfer both legs in one supported atomic operation

Read the Federal Reserve’s contract-inspection study for an empirical caution: public code and transaction visibility cover only part of a tokenized asset arrangement. Documentation, off-chain decisions and institutional responsibilities remain essential evidence. The practical test is whether a reviewer can trace one investor’s right from subscription through default, across both code and legal documents.

Hong Kong cases: property, custody and the airdropped injunction

A token is an entry in a computer system. A legal remedy identifies rights, parties and duties. The difficult work is connecting the two: showing which asset was lost, who controls the relevant wallet, what claim exists, and how a valid order reaches the people who can respond. The cases below teach different parts of that connection. They concern cryptocurrency custody, fraud and governance; they are not decisions approving every RWA structure.

Property and insolvency: Gatecoin, 2023 and 2025

Re Gatecoin Ltd (in liquidation) [2023] HKCFI 914, decided by Linda Chan J on 31 March 2023, confirmed that cryptocurrency is property capable of being held on trust. That does not mean every customer was a beneficiary. The court examined successive customer terms and their acceptance. A customer’s entitlement could therefore be proprietary or merely contractual. The judgment’s opening paragraphs explain that the immediate determination concerned trust status and specified assets, rather than completing every distribution question. [HKC1]

Re Gatecoin [2025] HKCFI 493, dated 24 January 2025, is the distribution sequel. The accessible commentary discusses non-consenting customers who retained rights under earlier trust terms. Read it as a continuation of a particular insolvency, not a universal rule that exchange customers rank above unsecured creditors. The full sequel judgment was not readable during this research session. [HKC2]

RWA application: ask what survives an issuer or platform insolvency. A statement that a token is “backed by assets” leaves unanswered who owns them, whether a trust exists, who bears a shortfall, and whether a security interest was perfected. The token balance alone cannot answer those questions.

USDT and platform promises: the JPEX default judgment

Chan Wing Yan and another v JP-EX Crypto Asset Platform Pty Ltd and others [2024] HKDC 1628, 29 October 2024, involved deposits of USDT. The accessible counsel headnote reports default judgment against the second defendant: the pleaded relationship established the three certainties of an express trust, supporting proprietary and ancillary injunctive relief. Its paragraph references place the trust analysis at paragraphs 20–31 and relief at 32–37. This was a decision on pleadings in default, not a contested trial deciding every victim’s entitlement or guaranteeing actual recovery. The official judgment link was inaccessible. [HKC4]

Stablecoin application: separate three relationships: the holder’s rights against the stablecoin issuer; the customer’s rights against the exchange; and the exchange’s control of wallets. A sound reserve arrangement at issuer level does not cure misconduct at an intermediary.

DAO governance remains accountable

MANTRA DAO Inc and another v John Patrick Mullin and others [2024] HKCFI 2099 arose in HCA 749/2022. Lok J made the decision on 25 April 2023 and issued reasons on 12 August 2024. Paragraph 1 records an interim order for financial spreadsheets from January 2021 and supporting documents to a limited group of advisers under confidentiality undertakings. A reproduction of the judgment is available in the ABI materials, beginning at printed page 282. This was financial disclosure during a disputed ownership and governance case, not a final declaration that token voting determines every ownership issue. [HKC3]

RWA application: identify the entity signing contracts and the individuals controlling keys. Calling a project a DAO does not identify the custodian, beneficiary, security trustee or person able to comply with a court order.

Three different on-chain service matters

Worldwide A-Plus Investments Ltd v A-Plus Meta Technology Ltd, HCA 2417/2024, is the 2024 Tron matter discussed by Joshua Chu and Christie Liu in March 2025. Their participant account describes minting an injunction-linked token and delivering on-chain notice to suspect wallets. Treat the account as evidence of an innovative procedure, not a published reasoned judgment establishing unlimited constructive notice. The article contains inconsistent-looking currency descriptions; this guide does not rely on its loss figure. [CHU1]

BP SG Investment Holding Ltd v Chen Shanxian and others, HCA 533/2025, is a separate matter. The Secretary for Justice’s official September 2025 speech describes April 2025 permission for tokenised service, wallet-to-wallet messaging and an NFT airdrop containing a hyperlink to the order. It also describes a direction to transfer suspected proceeds to custodial escrow pending the case. That is an official account of orders, not the full reasons or proof that every directed transfer was completed. [HKC6]

Yan Yu Ying v Persons Unknown and others, HCA 625/2025, provides another concrete service record. The public litigation site lists a 3 April 2025 inter partes injunction and subsequent orders. Searchable extraction from its order identifies Bitcoin Ordinal airdrop as a substituted-service method. The scanned PDF did not permit full text review, so no wider holding is asserted. A party’s service website is useful evidence of documents, but is not an official judgment database. [HKC7]

Keep four mechanisms separate: service delivers documents under the court’s permission; notice informs the recipient of a dispute; a legal freezing order restrains conduct; and technical immobilisation requires a person or system with actual control. An NFT arriving in a wallet does not itself disable its private key. A public ledger entry also does not automatically bind every overseas person or prove that a recipient read it. These distinctions are this guide’s analytical framework.

A limit on aggressive freezing: Wang Weiqing

Wang Weiqing v Zhuo Yihao and others [2025] HKCFI 4941, 17 October 2025, concerned an injunction affecting a Binance pooled hot wallet. The accessible judicial discussion by Justice Leeming explains that the wallet was used for numerous customers and relevant exchange correspondence had not been disclosed. The ex parte relief was discharged. Read the official discussion at pages 15–16; the Hong Kong full judgment was JavaScript-only during research. The lesson is the continuing importance of proper urgency, full and frank disclosure, and correct identification of the assets and controller. [HKC5]

Technical reading with Joshua Chu: arguments, architectures and limits

Read Joshua Chu’s work as a set of practitioner arguments about how law and software can operate together. Each text has a different date, purpose and evidential status. An article proposing a useful architecture is not itself a regulatory exemption, a judgment or an independently verified deployment report. The questions below connect his writing to a technical RWA and stablecoin review.

Documents, rights and tokens: the Ricardian approach

In Virtual Assets or Ricardian Contracts (December 2022), Chu and Macor Law describe a contract represented in human-readable legal language and machine-readable form. They present Digital Ownership Tokens as documentation linking an asset to ownership instruments in token metadata. Their central regulatory argument is that a documentation mechanism should be distinguished from a speculative token product. This is a useful conceptual distinction, written before later Hong Kong regulatory developments. It does not establish that every instrument called a DOT falls outside securities, collective-investment or virtual-asset rules. [CHU2]

Technical reading exercise: imagine a token referencing a signed warehouse receipt. Check whether the hash authenticates the actual operative document, where the document remains available, who signed it, whether transfer of the token assigns contractual rights, and whether the warehouse must recognise the transferee. A token can prove a file has not changed while the legal transfer remains incomplete. Hash integrity, legal validity and enforceability are three separate tests.

Programmable dispute resolution: smart awards

In Smart Contract Enabled Arbitral Awards (April 2023), Chu and Julian So propose automating performance following an award, for example by releasing escrowed funds. They also acknowledge code reliability, legal enforceability and unsuitable dispute types. Their model clause is a proposal for parties to consider, not a court-approved universal form. [CHU3]

Technical reading exercise: distinguish the arbitrator’s decision from the software instruction implementing it. A system needs an authenticated award input, identity checks, an amount and asset identifier, a challenge/stay process, and an authorised execution mechanism. It must specify what happens after an erroneous input or a successful challenge. If the asset is land or a bank account, execution also depends on the relevant registry or intermediary. The smart contract cannot grant itself control of an off-chain asset.

The tokenised injunction as a communication architecture

The March 2025 injunction article describes on-chain notice as a way of exposing disputes to counterparties and compliance systems. Its constructive-notice language is ambitious. Read it alongside the court-order accounts above, and ask whether a particular legal consequence follows from the actual order, recipient knowledge and applicable law. Participant experience is valuable, but a token’s visibility does not prove universal observation. [CHU1]

Technical reading exercise: document the chain, destination address, transaction hash, token identifier, metadata content, hosted document, timestamps and delivery evidence. Then test whether a normal wallet displays the token or hides it as spam. A robust service design should avoid demanding a signature or wallet connection merely to read the order. Notification success, readable content and technical freezing capability require different evidence.

Money’s legal foundation: Dollar vs Crypto

In Dollar vs Crypto (February 2026), Chu distinguishes sovereign money, CBDCs, privately issued stablecoins and speculative cryptocurrencies. The distinction between property status and legal-tender status is particularly useful. His wider claims about fiat value, government obligations and common-law versus civil-law preferences are interpretive generalisations, not a substitute for monetary law or a jurisdiction’s actual scheme. Do not infer a sovereign price guarantee from state issuance. [CHU4]

Technical reading exercise: for each settlement asset, identify the obligor, unit of account, redemption right, settlement finality and loss bearer. “One digital dollar” could be an issuer’s stablecoin liability, a bank deposit recorded on a new ledger, or central-bank money. The user interface may look similar while the creditor position differs substantially.

Cross-border compliance: useful theme, details to verify

MiCA’s Ripple Effect (May 2025), by Chu and Steven Gallagher, usefully warns that a Hong Kong platform’s business may engage more than one regulatory system. Read its detailed propositions critically. In particular, do not repeat its statement that Hong Kong VATPs must serve professional investors only, or treat all MiCA whitepapers as requiring ESMA approval. Product category, territorial activity and the applicable authority must be checked against current primary materials. Hong Kong’s 2026 authorised-fund secondary-trading framework expressly addresses public access; MiCA Article 8 distinguishes notification from prior approval for its specified category. [SFCSECOND] [ESMA] [CHU5]

Technical reading exercise: draw an actual distribution route: issuer, platform, marketing audience, investor location, custody, cash leg and asset situs. A smart contract accepting a wallet address does not decide whether a securities offer, licensing requirement, sanctions rule or Mainland restriction applies. Eligibility controls need a legal policy before engineers can encode them.

A combined review method

Use these articles to develop a six-layer analysis: identify the asset and cash flow; establish the enforceable legal claim; design the token and its transfer permissions; choose custody and settlement controls; specify disclosures and distribution; and prepare insolvency and dispute procedures. Work through each layer before accepting a claim that the product is “fully on-chain”.

For a tokenised rental-income note, that means asking who owns the building, who owes the payment, whether investors hold secured debt or a beneficial interest, how rent enters the payment waterfall, and who can enforce after default. For a stablecoin, it means asking who receives subscription money, where reserves sit, who can redeem, and what happens during a redemption queue. These are original teaching examples, not descriptions of a particular licensed product.

Seminar prompt: “The ledger shows a valid transfer, but the legal documents prohibit it. Who owns what?” A strong answer identifies both records, the governing terms, third-party rights, administrative powers and the available remedy. It does not stop at “code is law” or “the blockchain is immutable”.

FoundationLink

Explain the difficult ideas without jargon

Token ownership versus ownership of a building

Simple explanation: a token is like a named entry in an investment club’s record. The club’s rules decide what that entry gives you. A slice of the club is not necessarily a slice of the land.

Where the analogy fails: actual tokens can have automated transfer controls and may form part of the legally authoritative register.

Precise version: identify the legal instrument and authoritative record, then trace the investor’s claim through any company, trust or custody chain to the asset and enforcement rights.

A stablecoin peg

Simple explanation: a redeemable ticket can stay close to its promised cash value if people can actually exchange it at that value.

Where the analogy fails: real issuers have reserve portfolios, redemption restrictions and stress events; secondary buyers may not have identical access.

Precise version: primary-market convertibility and arbitrage may constrain secondary prices, but credit, liquidity, access and operational frictions can widen deviations.

Atomic settlement

Simple explanation: the machine swaps your payment for the investment only if both can be handed over together.

Where the analogy fails: the machine cannot automatically force an outside bank, registry or foreign court to treat the result identically.

Precise version: atomic execution conditions state changes within a defined technical domain; legal finality and off-chain performance require their own basis.

Serving an injunction through an airdrop

Simple explanation: the court allows notice to be delivered to a wallet that is linked to the defendant, much as a court might permit another unusual method when an address is unknown.

Where the analogy fails: wallet interfaces may hide unsolicited tokens; the exact authorised method and proof of delivery matter.

Precise version: service and notice are procedural matters. Any technical freeze or transfer depends on separate powers, control and orders. See the cases chapter.

Active recall and practice

Recall before you reveal

Twenty cards move from basic definitions to review questions. Say your answer first, then reveal. Use the previous/next buttons or Left/Right keys while the card area has focus. Space or Enter flips the card.

Sixteen exercises with worked answers

Five recall, six application and five synthesis questions. Write or speak an answer before opening the solution. These are original practice questions, not assessment questions supplied by BLF.

Recall 1. Define tokenization without using the word “crypto”.

Suggested answer: Representing or recording an asset or claim through a token on a programmable platform. Add the qualification that the legal rights depend on the structure and applicable law.

Recall 2. Name the three quantities that a stablecoin user should distinguish.

Suggested answer: The target reference price, actual secondary-market price and legal redemption amount/process. They can diverge in practice.

Recall 3. Which two Main­land documents must be read together for this guide’s 2026 RWA discussion?

Suggested answer: The eight-agency notice 银发〔2026〕42号 and the CSRC offshore domestic-asset ABS-token guidelines 公告〔2026〕1号. The older 2021 notice is historical.

Recall 4. What are the four different mechanisms involved in an on-chain injunction?

Suggested answer: Service, notice, legal restraint and technical immobilisation. An airdrop does not itself establish the fourth.

Recall 5. Give one difference between a stablecoin and a tokenized bank deposit.

Suggested answer: Identify a different obligor/legal relationship: a stablecoin issuer’s arrangement versus a bank deposit liability. Then check the actual terms rather than assuming identical protection.

Application 6. An issuer has 100m token liabilities, 20m cash and 80m securities. The securities realise 90% of their carrying value. A 30m redemption request arrives. Explain the two separate tests.

Suggested answer: Realised reserves are92m:72m securities plus20m cash. The model can pay30m by selling enough securities to raise10m, but has an8m deficit against all token liabilities. Immediate liquidity does not establish full solvency.

Application 7. A token promises 6% from rental property. List the questions before treating it as a property investment.

Suggested answer: Identify the owner, instrument, investor rights, mortgages, rental collection, fees, leverage, valuation, distributions, transfer restrictions and default/enforcement route. The token may be a company share or unsecured note, not land title.

Application 8. A Hong Kong SPV sells tokens linked to Mainland receivables. Is Hong Kong compliance enough?

Suggested answer: No. Analyse the domestic assets, participants, controlled offshore entities and cash flows under the 2026 Mainland texts, and all relevant filing/approval, foreign-exchange and data obligations. Then separately analyse the offshore offer and intermediaries.

Application 9. A normal wallet hides an injunction NFT as spam. What evidence and procedural questions matter?

Suggested answer: Read the order’s authorised service method, identify the destination and transaction, preserve metadata/document access and timing, and assess delivery/display evidence. Do not assume actual knowledge or automatic technical freezing.

Application 10. A fund token trades at 1.03 while its last NAV is 1.00 and the underlying market is closed. What could explain it?

Suggested answer: Different information, demand, liquidity-provider risk, stale NAV and access costs can create a premium. Check valuation timing, pricing alerts, dealing/redemption rules and market-maker obligations rather than assuming guaranteed arbitrage.

Application 11. An approved security-token buyer loses eligibility before settlement. Predict the toy DvP outcome.

Suggested answer: Under the illustrated atomic controls, neither leg transfers. Real implementations must define exactly when eligibility is checked and how suspensions, expiry and recovery interact.

Synthesis 12. Design a due-diligence pack for a tokenized private-credit product.

Suggested answer: Include asset/borrower verification, enforceable entitlement and perfection analysis, offering/licensing map, authoritative register, token/admin audit, custody/segregation, valuation/oracle policy, money settlement, servicing waterfall, default plan and recovery rehearsal. Explain how each piece closes a specific gap.

Synthesis 13. Compare a legal freeze of an exchange account with a freeze of a pooled hot wallet.

Suggested answer: The account may be an intermediary’s customer-level record. The hot wallet may contain many users’ assets. Correct tracing, notice, scope, third-party effects and full disclosure matter; Wang illustrates the danger of overbroad relief.

Synthesis 14. A project says “our smart contract implements the award automatically”. What safeguards would you demand?

Suggested answer: Authenticated award input, correct asset/amount/party mapping, lawful control, challenge and stay handling, error correction, role governance and off-chain enforcement provisions. Separate the legal decision from its software implementation.

Synthesis 15. Compare a 2-day netted market with an atomic gross-settlement market. Which is safer?

Suggested answer: There is no answer from speed alone. Compare principal/counterparty risk, prefunding, liquidity, netting, operational dependency, finality and legal enforceability under stress. State assumptions before ranking them.

Synthesis 16. Explain the two sessions in one short paragraph for a newcomer.

Suggested answer: An RWA token records a claim to an investment, while a stablecoin can supply a payment token for buying it. Both require a trustworthy legal and operational chain. Jurisdiction, custody, redemption, governance and remedies determine the outcome when the technology is working or failing.

Quick-reference sheet

Five questions for any product

  1. What is the legally enforceable claim?
  2. Who owes, holds and controls each asset?
  3. Which record establishes ownership?
  4. What money settles, and when is it final?
  5. Who can fix, freeze, redeem or enforce?

Five recurring mistakes

  1. Confusing a token with underlying title.
  2. Confusing backing with accessible redemption.
  3. Confusing a consultation with enacted law.
  4. Confusing continuous trading with liquidity.
  5. Confusing airdrop notice with technical freezing.

Three useful calculations

These are analytical identities, not product-specific rules. Net asset value per share means net assets divided by outstanding shares. Loan-to-value means loan principal divided by collateral value. A reserve coverage ratio compares realised reserve value with token liabilities. Valuation, fees and legal availability can change what the inputs mean.

\[\text{NAV per share}=\frac{\text{assets}-\text{liabilities}}{\text{shares outstanding}}\]
\[\text{LTV}=\frac{\text{loan principal}}{\text{collateral value}}\]
\[\text{reserve coverage}=\frac{\text{realised reserve value}}{\text{outstanding token liabilities}}\]

Example: a 10m loan against collateral valued at 20m has 50% LTV. If the collateral falls to 12.5m, LTV rises to 80% without any new borrowing. A tokenized collateral system needs a valuation policy and lawful enforcement process, not only a formula.
