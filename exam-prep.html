    Distinctions to remember

    Keep these apartThe question to ask
    Legal / beneficial / present enjoymentWho has title, who is entitled, and when can the asset be enjoyed?
    Vested / contingent / postponed enjoymentIs there a condition on entitlement, or merely delayed payment? Read the whole provision.
    Certainty / formalities / constitutionIs the arrangement sufficiently defined, evidenced as required, and completed by the intended method?
    Fixed / discretionary objectsComplete-list approach or individual-membership test?
    Purpose / charity / restricted paymentWhat is the particular route to enforceability and property consequences?
    Sole-name / joint-name homeWhat starting point applies, and what evidence could displace it?
    Acquisition / quantificationHas an interest been established before its size is decided?
    Trust / estoppelWhich elements and remedial inquiry belong to this route?
    Power / duty / loyaltyWas the act authorised, performed carefully, and free of an unauthorised conflict or profit?
    Loss / gain / propertyCompensation, account of profits, or an identified asset claim?
    Following / tracing / claimSame asset, substitute value, or the legal basis for recovery?
    Receipt / assistanceWas there beneficial receipt, or participation in a breach? Apply the right mental element.

    Practise how you reason

    For an original practice problem, use five short steps: issue → precise rule → facts supporting it → strongest counterargument or missing fact → reasoned conclusion and consequence. Spend most of the answer explaining why the relevant facts matter. A case name alone does not apply a rule.

    Four possible task types to rehearse

    • Interpret a legal provision: identify the interest or power created, any condition and each affected person. Explain genuine alternative readings.
    • Advise on a claim: choose the legal route, work through each element, identify evidence gaps and explain the practical outcome.
    • Evaluate a proposition: state the current rule, explain its rationale, test a serious objection and give a supported judgement.
    • Choose the best SBA option: identify the decisive fact, eliminate options using the wrong test and reject statements that turn a qualified rule into an absolute one.

    These are general skill categories, not a forecast of the university’s questions.

    Case-reading prompts

    • Jones v Kernott [2011] UKSC 53, paragraphs 51–52: compare the joint-name starting point with sole-name acquisition, then separate inference from imputation at the appropriate stage.
    • Guest v Guest [2022] UKSC 27: identify the remedial issue; compare the majority and dissent rather than extracting a slogan that every promise must be fulfilled in full.

    Before you finish revision

    • Explain each distinction above without looking, then test yourself on the applied questions.
    • For your weakest unit, complete one three-minute written exercise before opening its reasoning notes.
    • Have the current candidate instructions and the blank university Answer Booklet ready. Check your own applicable start time and submission arrangements.
    • In the written exam, follow the actual question and any subpart marks. Part A’s 60 marks do not make every subpart equally weighted.
    • State the word count at the top. The advance document says formal referencing of authorities is not required for this timed exam; that does not replace accurate use of legal principles.
    • Use your own assessment writing and follow the university’s AI rules. These practice results do not establish exam readiness or submission status.
