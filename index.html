    Quick rule sheet — the distinctions to retrieve from memory

    Equity & Trusts

    Creating a trust
    Intention + subject matter + objects; then formalities and constitution. A fixed trust’s complete-list test differs from a discretionary trust’s individual-membership test. Unit 2
    Family-home claims
    Check express declaration and legal title. Establish the interest under the correct doctrine before quantifying it; sole-name and joint-name starting points differ. Unit 4
    Care versus loyalty
    Care concerns the applicable standard and administration. Loyalty concerns conflicts and unauthorised profits; honest motives or no loss do not alone defeat it. Unit 5 · Unit 6
    Choose the response
    Loss → compensation with the required causal analysis. Gain → account of profits. Identified property → establish title, trace substitutes and consider proprietary relief and defences. No double recovery. Unit 7
    Third parties
    Knowing receipt: beneficial receipt, the proprietary requirement and unconscionable knowledge. Dishonest assistance: breach, assistance and dishonesty; receipt is unnecessary. Unit 8

    English Legal System & Constitutional Law

    Before using a case
    Court + ratio + material facts + binding status. Distinguishing, overruling and reversing do different work. Unit 2
    Before challenging a measure
    Classify it: Westminster Act, delegated legislation, executive act or devolved legislation. Find the power and legal limit before choosing a remedy. Units 3–6
    Law versus convention
    Recognition of a convention does not itself make it judicially enforceable. Identify any separate statutory rule. Unit 6
    HRA declaration
    Section 4 does not invalidate the provision. Separate interpretation, declaration and executive/public-authority duties. Unit 5
    EU-derived rules
    Start with the date, source and domestic gateway. Check preservation category, reform, commencement and any special withdrawal regime. Historical supremacy is not a complete present-day answer. Units 7–9

    Explain it simply, then precisely

    Worked approaches to fresh facts

    A trustee makes a poorly researched investment

    Facts: A trustee buys shares using trust money. The shares fall in value. The trustee did not consider diversification or obtain advice.

    Issue and rule: Separate the investment power from its exercise. Read the instrument, then the applicable Trustee Act 2000 provisions: reasonable care and skill, suitability, diversification, review and advice, including any proper exception.

    Application: A price fall alone proves little. Ask why the investment was suitable, what the portfolio already contained, what advice would have addressed and whether a reasonable basis existed for dispensing with it. Identify evidence rather than assume every omission caused the whole loss.

    Conclusion and remedy: State which duties may have been breached, then analyse the loss causally attributable to that breach, any exemption clause and section 61 relief. Compare AIB on compensation; do not turn a negligence problem into an automatic account of profits.

    An agent takes a secret commission and transfers its proceeds

    Issue and rule: Establish the fiduciary undertaking and the connection between the payment and that position. Check authorisation and fully informed consent. FHR supports a proprietary response to a fiduciary’s secret commission.

    Application: Against the agent, distinguish an account of the gain from compensation for loss. Follow the money into any substitute asset. Against a third party, consider separately whether a proprietary interest survives, whether knowing receipt is established, or whether the third party dishonestly assisted.

    Conclusion: Give a defendant-by-defendant answer. An innocent volunteer, a knowing recipient, a dishonest assistant and a bona fide purchaser are not interchangeable. Explain what additional facts are needed and avoid recovering the same value twice.

    A minister makes a rule restricting an activity

    Issue and rule: First establish whether this is a statutory instrument, another executive decision or an Act. For delegated legislation, read the parent Act’s scope, purpose and procedures.

    Application: Compare the restriction with the actual delegated power. If Convention rights arise, examine the relevant HRA route and whether primary legislation requires the incompatibility. Policy disagreement alone does not establish legal invalidity.

    Conclusion and remedy: Identify the legal ground and available response under the applicable scheme. If the problem instead concerns an incompatible Westminster Act, a section 4 declaration does not invalidate it.

    Connect the concepts

    Both subjects ask how a source of power creates limits, rights and remedies. The analogy helps organise revision; it does not merge the doctrines.

    The connections in words

    Source → power → limit → remedy
    For constitutional problems, identify the institution and legal source of power. For trust administration, identify the instrument or statutory power. Each needs a separate analysis of the duties or limits and the appropriate response.
    Trust validity → beneficial interest → proprietary recovery
    A proprietary remedy needs an identified legal basis. Certainty and constitution help establish the interest; tracing follows the value; defences and the law governing intervening transfers can affect recovery.
    Precedent → interpretation → application
    Across both subjects, knowing a case name is not enough. Identify the proposition, its binding status and the material similarities or differences before using it.
    Personal relief ≠ proprietary relief
    Personal liability concerns what a defendant must pay or do. Proprietary relief concerns an interest in identified assets or their substitutes. This difference matters especially in insolvency.
